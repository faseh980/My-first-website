Tax

Tax planning is a year-round job, not a deadline

By the time a return is due, most of the opportunities to reduce tax have passed. Here's how to plan ahead.

By [Author name][Publish date]4 min read

Many businesses think about tax only when a filing deadline is close. By then the year's transactions are done, and the options are limited to reporting them correctly. Real tax savings come from planning before decisions are made.

Plan major decisions before you make them

Buying equipment, hiring, entering a new market or changing your business structure can all change your tax position. A short review beforehand often reveals a better way to do the same thing.

Review the year at the half-way point

A mid-year projection of taxable profit shows whether advance tax payments are right, and leaves time to use allowances and incentives before year end.

Keep documentation as you go

Claims are only as strong as the evidence behind them. Good records of expenses, contracts and withholding tax deductions make filings faster and tax audits far less stressful.

Think about owners as well as the business

How profits reach the owners, through salary, dividends or other means, has a big effect on total tax paid. Business and personal tax planning work best together.

A one-hour planning session each quarter is usually enough to keep tax under control and avoid surprises.
