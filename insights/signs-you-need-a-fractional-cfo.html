FP&A & CFO

Five signs your business needs a fractional CFO

Growth creates financial complexity faster than most teams can handle. These are the warning signs that it's time for senior finance support.

By [Author name][Publish date]5 min read

Most businesses don't plan to hire a CFO. They reach a point where the finance function that worked at ten people stops working at fifty, and decisions start being made without good numbers. A fractional CFO gives you senior financial leadership for a few days a month, at a fraction of the cost of a full-time hire.

1. You're profitable but always short of cash

Profit and cash are not the same thing. Fast growth, slow-paying customers and stock build-ups can drain cash even when the income statement looks healthy. A CFO builds a rolling cash forecast so you can see problems months before they arrive.

2. Your numbers arrive too late to be useful

If management accounts arrive six weeks after month end, they describe history, not the business you're running today. A faster close and a short monthly dashboard change how you make decisions.

3. You're planning to raise money

Investors and lenders expect a credible financial model, clean historical numbers and someone who can answer detailed questions. Preparing these well can improve both your chances and your terms.

4. You can't tell which products or clients make money

Revenue growth can hide unprofitable work. Understanding margin by product, customer or location often reveals quick wins in pricing and cost.

5. The founder is still the finance department

When the owner signs every payment and builds every spreadsheet, finance becomes a bottleneck. A fractional CFO puts the right processes and people in place so you can focus on growth.

If two or more of these sound familiar, a short conversation with a CFO-level adviser usually pays for itself.
