What investors look for in financial due diligence

Due diligence can make or break a deal. Knowing what investors will test helps you prepare and protect your valuation.

By [Author name][Publish date]6 min read

Once an investor or buyer agrees a headline price, due diligence decides whether that price survives. Their advisers will test every assumption behind your numbers. Businesses that prepare in advance close faster and keep more of their valuation.

Quality of earnings

Investors want to know whether profits are sustainable. Expect questions about one-off items, owner-related expenses, revenue recognition and how much profit depends on a few customers.

Cash and working capital

A business can show profit and still consume cash. Diligence teams look at how cash converts from profit, seasonal patterns, and the normal level of working capital the business needs.

Debt and debt-like items

Loans are obvious, but unpaid taxes, deferred revenue, overdue suppliers and pending legal claims may also be deducted from the price.

The forecast

Your forecast must link clearly to historical performance. Aggressive assumptions without evidence reduce credibility across the whole business plan.

How to prepare

  • Close your books accurately and promptly for at least the last two to three years.
  • Build a clean data room with contracts, financial statements and tax filings.
  • Consider vendor due diligence to find and fix issues before buyers do.
Issues found by the buyer reduce your price. Issues you find and explain first rarely do.
